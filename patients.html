About our pain service

We are a tertiary referral service for children and young people living with persistent pain. We work as part of a multidisciplinary team — anaesthetists, clinical psychologists and physiotherapists — using a biopsychosocial approach to pain management.

Thoughts, feelings and behaviours are closely linked to how we experience pain, and psychological techniques have a strong evidence base for improving quality of life. We provide pain education, pain management strategies, and a range of non-pharmacological and alternative treatments. Medication is a minor part of most young people's pain management plan.

Our goal is to improve your level of function and quality of life — helping you get back to school, hobbies and time with friends and family. We also hope to achieve some reduction in pain itself, but we are honest from the outset that this can be hard to accomplish, so we try to avoid unrealistic expectations.

Before being referred, it really helps if you (and your family) are:
  • Beyond the investigation-seeking and diagnosis-seeking phase — happy to accept the pain diagnosis or pain formulation
  • Open to non-pharmacological approaches, pain education, physiotherapy and psychology
  • Motivated to engage with the team

There is often a waiting list for our clinics. The information on this page is designed to give you a head start — please have a read before your first appointment.

Before your appointment

Things to think about

It helps if you have had a chance to reflect on your pain before we meet. You don't need to write anything down, but thinking through these questions will make the first appointment much more useful.

  1. 1Where is your pain? What does it feel like — sharp, burning, aching, tight?
  2. 2What makes your pain better? Rest, movement, heat, certain positions, distraction?
  3. 3What makes your pain worse? Activities, time of day, stress, sleep?
  4. 4What have you already tried — medicines, physiotherapy, exercises, other approaches?
  5. 5How has pain affected your daily life — school, sleep, mood, relationships, hobbies?
  6. 6What would you most like to be able to do again, if pain improved?
